Experiment 1: Basis Set Incompleteness Error

How large does a basis set need to be?

The HCl dimer with four methods and six basis sets from two families.

At a glance. 2 molecules × 4 methods × 6 basis sets = 48 jobs, all short. You will learn what ζ means, how the error of a method changes as the basis grows, how two basis-set families compare, and how the cost grows with the number of basis functions.

Background

There are many different “families” of basis sets (Jensen 2013; Nagy and Jensen 2017), developed over the past sixty years by different computational chemists. Head-Gordon and co-workers (Witte et al. 2016) found that the most cost-effective basis sets for DFT belong to the Ahlrichs family (Weigend and Ahlrichs 2005). In this experiment you will analyze how they perform for the dissociation of the HCl dimer, taken from the NCCE30 subset (Peverati and Truhlar 2014; Marshall et al. 2011; Vydrov and Van Voorhis 2012; Lange and Lane 2011; McMahon and Lane 2011) of the Minnesota 2015B database (Yu et al. 2016). The reference energy for this dissociation is 2.01 kcal/mol.

You will calculate the dissociation energy with three functionals, B3LYP (Vosko et al. 1980; Becke 1988; Lee et al. 1988; Becke 1993; Stephens et al. 1994), PBE (Perdew et al. 1996) and ωB97M-V (Vydrov and Van Voorhis 2010; Mardirossian and Head-Gordon 2016), and with the Hartree–Fock (HF) method (Hartree 1928b, 1928a; Fock 1930; Slater 1930). You will use two families of basis sets:

  • the Ahlrichs def2-SVP, def2-TZVP and def2-QZVP basis sets (Weigend and Ahlrichs 2005);
  • the Dunning correlation-consistent basis sets (Dunning 1989; Woon and Dunning 1993, 1994; Wilson et al. 1999; Koput and Peterson 2002; Balabanov and Peterson 2005; Prascher et al. 2011), written cc-pVnZ, where “cc” means correlation consistent and n is D for double-ζ, T for triple-ζ or Q for quadruple-ζ.

For each method/basis combination, compute the reaction energy and its error with respect to the reference.

\[ \Delta E = 2\,E(\mathrm{HCl}) - E\big((\mathrm{HCl})_2\big) \]

\[ \varepsilon = \Delta E_\text{comp} - \Delta E_\text{ref} \]

TipTip

When performing calculations, errors arise from different sources. Each basis set carries an error because it is a truncated expansion. This is the basis set incompleteness error (BSIE; computational chemists love acronyms). To minimize its impact, run calculations with the largest basis set you can afford. Put differently, you want the error from the basis set to be small, so that the error from the exchange–correlation functional is always the dominant one.
