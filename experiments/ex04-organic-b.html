Experiment 4: Applying DFT to Organic Chemistry – Part B

Diels–Alder, alkane isomers and dispersion-bound dimers

Four more textbook reactions, where dispersion and changes in bonding start to matter.

At a glance. 10 molecules × 16 methods = 160 jobs with def2-TZVP. Most take seconds; the two hexanes with hybrid functionals take up to about 5 minutes each. You will learn when error cancellation stops helping, and what dispersion corrections (-D4) and nonlocal correlation (VV10) actually fix.

Background

In this experiment you will deal with four more organic reactions commonly found in textbooks:

  • 4a: the Diels–Alder reaction between ethene and butadiene to give cyclohexene, ΔEref = –45.40 kcal/mol.
  • 4b: the isomerization of n-hexane to neohexane (2,2-dimethylbutane), ΔEref = –2.49 kcal/mol.
  • 4c: the formation of the ethane dimer, held together only by van der Waals (dispersion) interactions, ΔEref = –1.34 kcal/mol.
  • 4d: the formation of the methanol dimer, held together by a hydrogen bond, ΔEref = –5.81 kcal/mol.

These structures come from the DARC (Goerigk et al. 2017; Goerigk and Grimme 2010; Johnson et al. 2008) and ADIM6 (Goerigk et al. 2017; Grimme et al. 2010) subsets of the GMTKN55 database (Goerigk et al. 2017), and from the AlkIsomer11 (Curtiss et al. 1997; Karton et al. 2009) and S66 (Řezáč et al. 2011a, 2011b) subsets of the MGCDB84 database (Mardirossian and Head-Gordon 2017).

As in Experiment 3, use SPW92, BP86, BLYP, PBE, PBE-D4, SCAN, B97M-V, B3LYP, B3LYP-D4, B3PW91, PBE0, M06-2X, M11, MN15, ωB97M-V and Hartree–Fock (references in Experiment 3), all with the def2-TZVP basis set (Weigend and Ahlrichs 2005). For each functional, compute the reaction energies and the errors with respect to the reference.

Note

For the dimers (4c, 4d) the reaction is monomers → dimer, so a negative ΔE means the dimer is bound. In 4d the two methanol monomers have slightly different geometries (they are cut from the dimer), so compute both.
