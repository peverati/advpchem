Experiment 6: Why is the B3LYP/6-31G* Level of Theory so Successful?

A famous method and its lucky error cancellation

Background

The B3LYP functional (Vosko et al. 1980; Becke 1988; Lee et al. 1988; Becke 1993; Stephens et al. 1994) with the 6-31G* basis set of Pople and co-workers (Ditchfield et al. 1971; Hehre et al. 1972; Hariharan and Pople 1973; Dill and Pople 1975; Binkley and Pople 1977; Francl et al. 1982; Gordon et al. 1982; Rassolov et al. 1998, 2001) is the de facto standard method in the organic chemistry community. The functional is based on the three-parameter hybrid scheme Becke introduced in 1993 (Becke 1993). The original three-parameter functional combined his 1988 exchange functional (Becke 1988) with the Perdew–Wang (PW91) correlation functional (Perdew and Wang 1992, 2018; Perdew et al. 1992). One year later, Frisch and co-workers introduced B3LYP by replacing PW91 with LYP (Lee et al. 1988), which gave better results in a computational study of circular dichroism spectra (Stephens et al. 1994). B3LYP became more popular than B3PW91, even though their performance and theoretical foundations are almost identical.

6-31G* is a double-ζ split-valence polarized basis set, so it contains a moderate number of basis functions. Its use with B3LYP became popular in the 1990s and early 2000s because it allowed calculations on relatively large molecules relevant to organic chemistry, with performance far better than the methods available before, such as semi-empirical methods and Hartree–Fock. Given how much computers have improved in the last two decades, however, a double-ζ basis set in a research setting is sometimes dangerous, as you saw in Experiment 1.

In this experiment you will test B3LYP with several double-ζ polarized basis sets to understand why B3LYP/6-31G* is so “special”:

  • 6-31G* (Pople)
  • cc-pVDZ (Dunning) (Dunning 1989; Woon and Dunning 1993, 1994; Wilson et al. 1999; Koput and Peterson 2002; Balabanov and Peterson 2005; Prascher et al. 2011)
  • def2-SVP (Ahlrichs) (Weigend and Ahlrichs 2005)
  • pc-1 (Jensen) (Jensen 2001, 2002, 2007, 2012; Jensen and Helgaker 2004). If it is not available, use def2-SV(P), the smallest of the Ahlrichs basis sets.

Run them on three reactions you already know: the Diels–Alder reaction 4a, the alkane isomerization 4b and the CaO dissociation 5b. They were chosen because they respond to the small basis sets in very different ways; find out how. Compare with the def2-TZVP results you obtained in Experiments 4 and 5 with the same functionals. As before, report each computed reaction energy with its error with respect to the reference value.

TipTip

This experiment shows that B3LYP/6-31G* works so well because it benefits from a fortuitous error cancellation (Kruse et al. 2012). Theory says a functional’s error with a small basis set should always be larger than with a big one. That B3LYP/6-31G* doesn’t follow this expectation when compared with B3LYP/def2-TZVP is worrisome: it points to a lucky cancellation of errors. Lucky cancellations are unpredictable. Sometimes they work in your favor, as for the reactions of Experiment 4, and sometimes against you, as for those of Experiment 5. The 6-31G* basis set does not make B3LYP better or more physically sound. Instead of trying to work out which chemical problems B3LYP/6-31G* can solve, we suggest moving away from it toward a more modern functional with predictable behavior with respect to the basis set. If that’s not possible because of limited computing resources, put more appropriate corrections in place, such as those suggested by Kruse, Goerigk and Grimme, with the help of an expert. In 2012, Cohen, Mori-Sánchez and Yang wrote that uniformly outperforming B3LYP was still a challenge for xc functionals (Cohen et al. 2012). Recent reviews have shown that this is no longer true (Mardirossian and Head-Gordon 2017; Goerigk and Mehta 2019; Peverati and Truhlar 2014; Yu et al. 2016). We feel the real challenge now is to get rid of B3LYP/6-31G* altogether, though, as Kruse, Goerigk and Grimme noted, we don’t expect this to happen anytime soon.
